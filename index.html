    How it works

    A rapid innovation challenge, not a hackathon.

    You are judged on the problem you choose and the solution you propose — not on what you can build in sixty minutes.

    Three or four people

    Three is the minimum, four the maximum. The fourth member is optional.

    Roles follow the order

    Member 1 leads, 2 makes the deck, 3 researches. A fourth picks one of two more.

    Two or three slides, cutoff at 3:38 pm

    Problem, solution, innovation, impact, feasibility — combined across the slides. About 30 seconds a slide when you pitch. Late decks are not judged.

    Laptop or phone

    Whichever you work faster on. A phone is enough for all of it. Come charged — sockets are not guaranteed.

    Any AI app you like

    For research and for building the slides. Closed once the presentations begin.

    Nothing prepared in advance

    You pick the problem on the day. A pre-built deck removes a team.

    Minute by minute

    Splitting the hour

    Six blocks between 3:00 and 4:00. Orange means AI is allowed; red means it is closed.

    Organisers: the pitch block is the constraint

    One stage, one panel, every team in turn. 13 teams at 90 seconds each is 19.5 minutes of pitching in a 22-minute block — 150 seconds spare for changeover across the whole running order.

    So each team gets 1:41 of the clock — 1:30 of talking plus about 11 seconds to swap the deck and walk up. That only holds if every file is already loaded on the podium machine, which is what the submission block at 3:36 is for. Teams plugging in their own laptops will cost thirty seconds each and the last team will not pitch.

    The pitch is 90 seconds rather than two minutes for the same reason: at two minutes these teams need 26 minutes of the hour, and the blocks that produce the idea collapse to a scramble.
