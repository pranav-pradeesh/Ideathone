    How it works

    A rapid innovation challenge, not a hackathon.

    You are judged on the problem you choose and the solution you propose — not on what you can build in sixty minutes.

    Three or four people

    Three is the minimum, four the maximum. The fourth member is optional.

    Roles follow the order

    Member 1 leads, 2 makes the deck, 3 researches. A fourth picks one of two more.

    Three or four slides, cutoff at 3:40 pm

    Problem, solution, innovation, impact, feasibility. Late decks are not judged.

    Laptop or phone

    Whichever you work faster on. A phone is enough for all of it. Come charged — sockets are not guaranteed.

    Any AI app you like

    For research and for building the slides. Closed once the presentations begin.

    Nothing prepared in advance

    You pick the problem on the day. A pre-built deck removes a team.

    Minute by minute

    Splitting the hour

    Six blocks between 3:00 and 4:00. Orange means AI is allowed; red means it is closed.

    Organisers: the pitch block is the constraint

    13 teams at 2 minutes each is 26 minutes of pitching, against a 16-minute block. One after another does not fit in the hour. The pitches run as 2 parallel pods of 7, each with its own judging panel scoring the same rubric — 14 minutes a pod, which fits with changeover to spare.

    Only the pitch block needs separating — the first 44 minutes fit in one hall. If you would rather keep a single stage, the alternatives are 90-second pitches (20 minutes, still over) or shortlisting on the submitted decks — both change the format, so decide before the day.

    Rule 1.5

    Five roles, assigned by position

    Nobody picks the first three — they follow the order you register in. Only a fourth member has a choice, between the last two.

    AI policy

    AI is a tool here, not a contestant

    Use it for research and slides. The two blocks that decide the winner — choosing the idea, and the pitch — are AI-free.
