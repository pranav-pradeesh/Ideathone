How it works

An hour is not a hackathon. It is a decision drill.

You will not build a product in sixty minutes, and you are not being asked to. You are being judged on whether you can read a problem, choose one idea over three others, and defend that choice under questioning. Everything in the format below exists to protect those three things from the clock.

Maximum three members

Three is the cap, and it is a hard one. Four people cannot reach agreement inside a five-minute decision block — the format breaks before the idea does. One and two-person teams are welcome and have their own role fallbacks.

Every member has one named role

Agree your roles before you walk in, not in the first ten minutes. Each role has its own minute-by-minute in the role book, so nobody spends the opening block deciding who does what.

Five slides, hard cutoff

Problem, Idea, How it works, Impact, Ask. Submission closes at minute 48 and late decks are not judged. The cutoff is enforced by a clock, not by a person you can negotiate with.

AI for research and slides

Permitted, openly, in the phases where it saves real time. Closed in the two blocks that are actually being judged — the decision and the pitch. Full policy below.

Judged on four things

Clarity of the problem, originality of the idea, feasibility of the how, and the quality of your answers under questioning. Slide polish is worth nothing on its own.

No prior preparation

The problem statement is revealed at minute zero. Bringing a pre-built deck is the one thing that gets a team removed rather than marked down.

Minute by minute

Splitting the hour

Seven blocks, sixty minutes, no slack. The purple blocks are where AI tools are permitted; the red blocks are where they are closed. Your team's timer starts at the brief and does not stop.

Organisers: the pitch block is the constraint

Twelve minutes of pitching at three minutes per team fits 4 teams. That is the real capacity of a single room — every team beyond that either overruns the hour or does not pitch. Run parallel pods with a judge each, then a shortlist round afterwards if you need one.

The role book

Three roles, chosen before you arrive

Each member takes exactly one role, and no two members of a team hold the same one. Settle it before you arrive. Short-handed teams merge roles using the published fallbacks — there is no improvising on the day.

AI policy

AI is a tool here, not a contestant

Use it for research and for making the presentation — that is what it is good at, and pretending otherwise would just push it under the table. The two blocks that decide the winner are AI-free, because those are the blocks that are actually you.

      Disclosure is mandatory

      Why the Decide block is AI-free

      If a model picks the idea, the judges are marking the model. The five minutes between 15:00 and 20:00 are the one part of the hour where the team has to back its own judgement — so laptops close, the team argues out loud, and the Lead writes one sentence down. Judges will ask in Q&A why you chose this idea over the alternatives. Answer that well and the rest of the pitch gets easier.

      Registration is open

      Your branch, a team name, and who is on the team. That is the whole entry requirement — it takes under a minute.
